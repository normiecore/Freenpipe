# W3-11 open questions

Capture loop hardening A: loop stages and the wedge watchdog (vision-capture-wedge-watchdog),
probe timeouts (vision-probe-timeouts), fail-closed window exclusions
(vision-exclusion-unknown-fail-closed) and the corrupt-frame guard (vision-corrupt-frame-guard). Each
decision is the worker's call; the owner can overturn any of them.

## 1. Watchdog thresholds and restart limits

- **Question:** the spec infers the thresholds (wedge after 120 s without a terminal outcome, stall
  after a 90 s heartbeat, 5 min cool-down per monitor) and gives no overall limit.
- **Decision:** wedge 120 s, stall 90 s. After a restart the same monitor waits 5 min before the next
  one, and the wait doubles with every further restart of that monitor (10, 20, 40 min, capped at
  60 min); two hours without a restart of that monitor start it over at 5 min. Across all monitors
  at most 3 restarts run in any hour. A finding that has to wait is logged once at `info`, then at
  `debug`. A finding restarts nothing until a later pass, at least 3 s after the first sighting,
  still finds that monitor stuck (every pass in between included).
- **Reason:** the coordinator asked that a restart can never loop. A capture backend that is broken
  for good (a driver that hangs every new session) then costs at most three restarts an hour, while a
  one-off hang is cured within about two minutes. The second pass rules out the pass that runs right
  after the computer wakes from sleep: Rust leaves open whether `Instant` counts suspended time on
  Windows, and if it does every heartbeat and attempt age looks hours old until the loops move again,
  which they do within a second.

## 2. What counts as an answered capture attempt

- **Question:** the spec calls a still-attempting wedge "no terminal outcome (persisted / dedup-skip /
  dropped)". Is an attempt abandoned at the 15 s capture deadline a terminal outcome?
- **Decision:** no. An attempt is answered when a frame is written, skipped (dedup, window filter,
  throttles), rejected as corrupt, stored without pixels, or fails with an error. An attempt cut off
  at its deadline is not an answer. A still-attempting wedge is: the loop keeps turning, attempts keep
  starting (one is open, or the last started within 120 s), and attempts have gone unanswered for
  120 s, counted from the first attempt that started after the last answer (not from the last answer
  itself, so the first capture after an hour out of focus is not mistaken for a wedge while it runs).
  While W2-13's write queue reports
  itself degraded the finding is logged at `debug` and nothing restarts, because a failing database
  writer explains timeouts and a restart cannot fix it.
- **Reason:** the capture call runs under a 15 s deadline, so an attempt never stays open long enough
  to look stuck; the loop heartbeat keeps turning, so the gone-silent rule never fires either. A
  wedged capture session (the Windows Graphics Capture case the owner cares most about) therefore
  shows up as nothing but deadline overruns, and that is exactly what a fresh capture session cures.

## 3. What a restart does, and how it fits W2-11, W2-26 and W3-30

- **Question:** the spec says the watcher "stops then starts the VisionManager". How does that sit
  with the per-monitor pauses, the engine watchdog and the low-disk guard?
- **Decision:** the monitor watcher runs the watchdog once per 5 s pass, after its DRM, schedule and
  start-retry handling, only while the manager is `Running`. A restart calls the new
  `VisionManager::stop_for_restart` (the body of `stop()` minus the focus-tracker shutdown), waits
  up to 5 s for the old loops to unregister from the stage tracker, then calls `start()`; the
  watcher skips the rest of that pass and the next pass re-reads the monitors as usual. Never
  judged: monitors the user paused (W2-11; they have no capture task and `start()` leaves them
  idle), monitors without a running task, loops in `pause-gate` or `cold-wait`, and the whole
  pipeline while the screen is locked, DRM content is focused or the schedule is outside work hours.
  Power-profile pauses park the loop in `pause-gate`. The low-disk guard (W3-30) stops capture through
  the app's `stop_capture`, which aborts the monitor watcher (and with it the watchdog) before it
  shuts the manager down, so a guard stop can never be undone by a restart. W2-26's watchdog
  respawns the whole engine when `/health` stops answering; this one only restarts the vision capture
  tasks of an engine that answers, so the two never act on the same symptom.
- **Reason:** `stop()` and `start()` already serialise with pause and resume through W2-11's monitor
  lock, and `start_monitor` refuses to add a second task for a monitor, so the restart cannot leave
  two capture loops on one monitor. A task stuck in synchronous code only unwinds at its next await;
  its stage cell carries an ownership token, so it can neither move nor remove its replacement's
  cell, and it is cancelled the moment it yields. Keeping the focus tracker matters because
  `stop()` shuts it down for good: after a plain stop and start every monitor would be captured at
  the full focused rate until the app restarts.

## 4. The diagnostic and the analytics event

- **Question:** the target sends a `vision_capture_wedge_restart` analytics event with
  `attempt_age_secs` and `loop_heartbeat_age_secs`.
- **Decision:** no event. One `warn` line names the monitor and class (`still-attempting wedge` or
  `gone-silent stall`), says `status=Running`, gives the stage and how long it has been frozen (or
  that no stage was ever entered), the loop heartbeat age, the last attempt age and how long there
  has been no answered capture, and says the VisionManager is being restarted. Follow-up lines cover
  a failed stop, loops that had not unwound after the 5 s wait, a successful restart and a failed
  start (the watcher then keeps retrying as it does after any failed start).
- **Reason:** no phoning home (CLAUDE.md); the log keeps every fact the event carried.

## 5. Which stages are marked where

- **Question:** W2-13 marked `heartbeat`, `warm-wait`, `cold-wait`, `pause-gate`, `trigger-wait`,
  `visual-probe` and `capture` and left the rest to this WP.
- **Decision:** `focus-gate` around the focus-state query; `release-stream` around every
  `release_capture_stream()` (Cold edge, pause entry, corrupt-frame runs); `invalidate-streams` around
  the macOS post-unlock invalidation; `exclusion-probe` around every exclusion refresh (inside the
  capture too, which then returns to `capture`); `hot-cache-push` around the hot frame cache push;
  `trigger-drain` around the corr-id drains (Cold, pause, DRM-blocked trigger). Each stage cell now
  also records the last heartbeat, the open attempt, the last attempt and the last answered attempt
  for the watchdog. `/health` keeps W2-13's rule: the loop that has been in its stage the longest.
- **Reason:** these are the places a loop can block; a frozen loop then names where it froze.

## 6. Where the exclusion state can be unknown

- **Question:** fail closed when "exclusion state is unknown". Which platforms have one?
- **Decision:** only where capture can leave single windows out by id (ScreenCaptureKit on macOS)
  and the user has ignore, include or URL rules. There the list is built by enumerating windows on
  the blocking pool under a 2 s deadline, and it starts unknown, so nothing is captured before the
  first good refresh. On Windows and Linux, or with no rules, the list is empty by construction and
  always known; no enumeration runs. Ignored and private windows on those platforms stay protected
  the way the base protects them: the accessibility-based window gate skips the frame when the
  focused window matches a rule.
- **Reason:** Windows Graphics Capture and xcap capture whole monitors, so there is no per-window
  list whose staleness could leak pixels; inventing an enumeration there would add a failure mode
  without protecting anything.

## 7. What "pixels are withheld" means

- **Question:** "frame pixels are withheld until a refresh succeeds; a11y-only capture can continue".
- **Decision:** while the list is unknown no screenshot is taken at all: no snapshot file, no OCR, no
  change-probe screenshot, no HD chunk (macOS), no update of the change-detector baseline. The
  accessibility walk and every gate still run, and the frame row is stored with an empty
  `snapshot_path`, the accessibility text and `text_source` `accessibility`. Every capture refreshes
  the list first. A refresh that overran its deadline keeps its blocking thread; while it still hangs
  no second enumeration starts and the state stays unknown, and an answer that arrives late is
  discarded. The first unknown refresh logs a `warn`, later ones `debug`, and the first good refresh
  logs how long pixels were withheld. A known list is reused only by the Warm (unfocused) probe,
  whose image is diffed and never stored; a capture it triggers refreshes the list again.
- **Reason:** a stale list could miss a window the user asked to keep out that appeared since the
  last good refresh, so it is never reused for pixels that are kept. Storing the accessibility row
  keeps search and the timeline continuous during a window-server hang.

## 8. Probe timeouts

- **Question:** the spec suggests 3 s for the change probe and 2 s for the exclusion refresh.
- **Decision:** those values. The change-probe screenshot runs in its own task; on timeout the check
  is skipped, the loop goes on, the late task is kept, and no second probe starts while it runs (the
  check reports itself busy). Dropping the probe (the loop ends) aborts the late task. A probe that
  times out logs a `warn` once per run of timeouts and an `info` when probes answer again.
- **Reason:** one stuck task per monitor at most, however long the backend hangs.

## 9. Black frames: how strict

- **Question:** the base dropped "mostly black" frames (over 95 % of a 15x15 grid with an RGB sum
  below 15). The coordinator asked that no legitimately dark frame is dropped.
- **Decision:** a frame is corrupt-black only when every pixel has all channels at or below 2 (or the
  image has no pixels). A 64x64 grid finds candidates cheaply; a candidate is confirmed by scanning
  the whole frame, which stops at the first lit pixel. The base check and its tests are removed.
- **Reason:** never-written buffers and windows the OS blanked out are exactly zero. Real dark screens
  are not: a black terminal with one prompt line, an OLED-black interface with a clock, a dim video
  scene. The base threshold dropped a black terminal whose prompt fell between grid points, and with
  it the accessibility text of that frame. One consequence: on macOS, a frame where an ignored window
  covers all but a menu bar or dock is now stored; its excluded pixels are black either way.

## 10. Green decode garbage: what is a band

- **Question:** the evidence shows a green decode-garbage *band*, not a whole green frame, and no
  thresholds.
- **Decision:** a pixel is garbage green when red and blue are at most 4 and green falls in one of
  two narrow windows, 74-90 or 132-139. Those hold the values zeroed YUV converts to, worked out from
  the standard coefficients: about 77 (BT.709 limited range), 84 (BT.709 full), 89 (BT.2020
  limited) and 135-136 (BT.601 limited and full), with a few levels of slack for rounding. A grid
  row is garbage when at least 97 % of its
  64 samples are; a frame is corrupt-green when garbage rows form a full-width band covering a quarter
  of the frame height anywhere, or an eighth when the band touches the top or bottom edge (where an
  unfilled buffer tail shows up). A whole green frame is the largest such band.
- **Reason:** real interfaces with green (accent taskbars, spreadsheet headers, success banners,
  chroma-key screens) either do not span the full width, are thinner than the band, or carry red or
  blue; the tests cover each case. Flat pure greens are real content too: the solitaire felt and
  CSS `green` (0,128,0) and CSS `darkgreen` (0,100,0) cover half a maximized screen. A first
  version used one wide window (64-160) and rejected them, dropping the whole capture with its
  accessibility text and reporting a false stall; review round 1 narrowed it to the derived values,
  and the tests now include a maximized felt and a `darkgreen` page. Full-range BT.2020 (about 94)
  is left out: it would sit next to `darkgreen`, and HDR output reaches the capture path already
  converted to RGB.

## 11. What happens to a corrupt frame

- **Question:** the spec says corrupt frames are not stored, not compared and counted, and proposes
  releasing the persistent stream after N corrupt frames (marked as having no evidence).
- **Decision:** the whole capture is rejected before the accessibility walk: nothing is stored (not
  even accessibility text), the change-detector baseline is kept, and `frames_corrupt_black` or
  `frames_corrupt_green` goes up. Both count in `silent_loss`'s accounting as an outcome. A black
  frame also counts as a landing, the way a dedup skip does (it advances `last_db_write_ts`), so
  `/health` stays healthy while the OS blanks the screen on purpose. Decode-garbage green is not a
  landing: a monitor that only delivers garbage is reported stalled, with the cause
  `backend_rejecting`. Triggers of a rejected capture are released to the frame linker as
  `CaptureError`. After 3 corrupt frames in a row the monitor's persistent capture
  stream is released, so the next capture opens a fresh session, and while the run goes on it is
  released again after every 30 further corrupt frames (frames 3, 33, 63, ...), so a session that
  keeps returning zeroed buffers after a GPU reset recovers without a restart (review round 1);
  change-probe screenshots join
  the run (so a stream stuck on garbage recovers while only probes run) but are not counted in the
  metrics, which count capture attempts.
- **Reason:** a frame with no screen content has no trustworthy context either. Windows Graphics
  Capture returns an all-black frame for a full-screen protected video, the secure desktop (UAC) and
  some lock transitions, and macOS blanks a window left out of the capture; base already counted its
  "mostly black" skips as dedup landings, and calling those a stall would raise false "capture
  stalled" alarms for a whole film. Green garbage never comes from the OS on purpose, so hiding it
  from `/health` would hide a broken backend. The stream release is kept as a proposal because it is
  cheap, bounded (one reopen per 30 corrupt frames once a run is under way) and the only recovery
  short of the watchdog, which never sees a black run: black frames are answered attempts and
  landings. The cost for a screen the OS blanks on purpose for a whole film is one session reopen
  per 30 probe or capture frames.

## 12. Files outside the owner paths

- **Decision:** small hooks, each needed for the items above:
  - `crates/screenpipe-screen/src/metrics.rs` (W2-13's): per-loop liveness in the stage tracker
    (ownership token, heartbeat, open, last and last answered attempt), corrupt frames in
    `AttemptOutcomes` and `silent_loss`, `lib.rs` re-exports.
  - `crates/screenpipe-screen/src/capture_screenshot_by_window.rs`:
    `try_get_excluded_sck_window_ids` (an enumeration failure is an error, not an empty list),
    `window_filters_are_empty`, `WINDOW_ID_EXCLUSION_SUPPORTED`.
  - `crates/screenpipe-capture/src/paired_capture.rs`: the `pixels_withheld` field of
    `CaptureContext` and two conditions (no snapshot, no OCR when it is set). The OCR condition is the
    `if` line just above W3-12's OCR call region; the call itself is untouched, so a merge with W3-12
    should keep both edits.
  - `crates/screenpipe-engine/src/hd_recorder.rs` (macOS only, not compiled in the VM): the HD stream
    uses the same fail-closed exclusion probe per chunk.
  - `crates/screenpipe-engine/src/vision_manager/manager.rs` (W2-11's): `stop_for_restart`, a metrics
    accessor and two test helpers; `vision_manager/mod.rs` and `lib.rs` module lines.
- **Reason:** listed for the reviewer and the merger to check against the owning WPs.

## 13. Follow-ups left open

- A change probe that keeps timing out on an unfocused (Warm) monitor is skipped, not escalated:
  that monitor makes no capture attempts, so neither watchdog class fires until a capture on it hangs
  too. Counting a long-busy probe as a wedge would need the probe state in the stage tracker.
- The monitor watcher's own monitor enumeration has no deadline; if it hangs, the watchdog (which
  runs in the watcher) stops too.
